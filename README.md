# recurrent-tonsillitis-children-2025
A study analyzing the social determinants of health influencing recurrent tonsillitis in preschool and school-age children. Based on international survey data, the research explores the impact of socioeconomic status, hygiene, and childcare conditions on disease recurrence and prevention.

🔬 Abstract  
This study investigates the social determinants of health and their influence on the incidence of recurrent tonsillitis in preschool and school-age children. A total of 82 participants from Germany, the United Kingdom, Luxembourg, Ukraine, and Thailand were surveyed to examine how socioeconomic status, housing conditions, and childcare environments impact disease frequency. The results show a significantly higher recurrence rate of tonsillitis among children from low-income families, primarily due to restricted access to healthcare and suboptimal living standards. Furthermore, in overcrowded childcare groups (more than 20 children), recurrence reached 48%. The study highlights the necessity of preventive measures such as routine check-ups, maintaining hygiene standards, and educating parents about isolating symptomatic children. Recommendations are provided to support public health strategies aimed at reducing recurrent tonsillitis among young populations.
